# Contributing to Cubrim Research

Use English for maintained documents, issue briefs and research reports. Preserve source quotations and names accurately. This knowledge collection is separate from the commercial implementation.

## A useful contribution

Provide a specific research question; primary sources with authors/version/date; relevant assumptions; proposed method or reproducible evidence; data/figure rights; and limitations. Identify whether the item is a literature review, hypothesis, model, experiment or publication candidate. Do not substitute a polished narrative for missing measurements.

Literature reviews describe what each cited source actually establishes and what is inferred. An unsuccessful experiment can be valuable when its method and limits are documented. Do not claim novelty, universal superiority, implementation support or customer adoption without corresponding evidence.

## Publication process

Draft -> scientific/product review -> technical disclosure and rights check -> approved public artifact -> website article. Name actual authors and contributors; do not assign authorship to everyone who reviewed a draft. Preserve version history and link corrections to the affected publication.

Only rights-cleared data descriptions, figures and text enter the public repository. Do not upload confidential source, unreleased optimization designs, internal agent transcripts, customer information, security findings with exploit details or credentials. Use the project's private reporting channel for sensitive material.

No automated mirror from a private repository, no shared `.git` history and no unrestricted database dump. Review generated files, notebooks and embedded attachments as content, not just their extensions. Contributions to public research do not grant access or rights to private product source.

## Reuse terms

Each publication/data contribution needs an explicit rights and reuse record. A repository-wide reuse license is a separate owner decision; none is inferred from the public repository name. Preserve third-party notices. Research text and compiled-product licensing are separate questions.

## Article template

Title; question and context; source basis; method and assumptions; result or proposed experiment; limitations; implications for further research; references; authors/contributors; version and correction history.

Public content should explain the scientific issue clearly without revealing proprietary implementation. Keep links to publicly accessible evidence where available; label restricted evaluation materials rather than publishing access tokens or internal paths.
